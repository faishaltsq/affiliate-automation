## Understanding Your Power Needs: Capacity and Portability

Before you buy, ask yourself: *What devices will I charge, and how often?* Capacity is measured in milliampere-hours (mAh). For reference:

- **5,000–10,000 mAh** – Ideal for one full smartphone charge. Ultra-compact, fits in a coin pocket. Best for quick top-ups.
- **10,000–20,000 mAh** – The sweet spot for most users. Charges a phone 2–4 times. Still pocketable for most bags.
- **20,000–30,000 mAh** – Perfect for travelers or families. Can charge a tablet or multiple phones. Heavier, but still carry-on friendly.
- **30,000+ mAh** – Heavy-duty power for laptops and multiple devices. Usually larger and heavier; best for road trips or camping.

Portability is a trade-off. A 10,000mAh charger might weigh 200g, while a 30,000mAh unit can top 600g. Consider your daily carry: if you’re a commuter, a slim 10,000mAh model is plenty. If you’re a digital nomad working from cafes, a 20,000mAh unit with laptop charging capability is a better fit.

## Key Specs to Look For: Speed, Ports, and Safety

Not all portable chargers are created equal. Pay attention to these technical details:

- **Charging Protocols** – Look for USB-C Power Delivery (PD) for iPhones, iPads, and most modern laptops. Qualcomm Quick Charge (QC) is common for Android devices. Some chargers support both, plus Samsung’s Super Fast Charging. Avoid “standard” 5V/1A outputs—they’re painfully slow.
- **Number and Type of Ports** – A good charger has at least two USB-A ports and one USB-C port. The USB-C should support bidirectional charging (both input and output). Bonus points for a built-in AC wall plug or wireless charging pad.
- **Input Speed** – How fast can the charger itself recharge? Look for USB-C input with at least 18W (ideally 30W+). A 20,000mAh charger with 60W input can refill in under 2 hours.
- **Safety Features** – Overcharge protection, temperature control, short-circuit prevention, and flame-retardant casing are non-negotiable. Certifications like UL, CE, and FCC indicate rigorous testing.

## How to Maintain and Extend the Life of Your Portable Charger

A portable charger is an investment. To keep yours performing for years:

- **Avoid extreme temperatures** – Don’t leave it in a hot car or freezing backpack. Lithium-ion cells degrade fastest at high heat.
- **Store at 50–80% charge** – If you won’t use it for a month, keep it partially charged. Storing fully drained or fully charged long-term shortens lifespan.
- **Use the right cable** – Cheap cables can bottleneck fast charging. Use a certified USB-C to USB-C cable rated for 100W if your charger supports it.
- **Recharge regularly** – Even if you don’t use it, give it a full charge every 3 months to maintain cell health.
- **Keep it clean** – Dust and lint in the ports can cause poor connections. Blow them out gently with compressed air.

With proper care, a quality portable charger can last 3–5 years—far longer than the average smartphone.
